# Naming the agent — 32 Sanskrit-rooted names, tested against what it actually does

The product: a person types in a business idea → the agent tells them whether it fits their
culture, what worked for others, what killed the lookalikes, and what to test first.

A name for this must pass **four tests**:

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Say it** | Can a non-Indian say it correctly on the first try? Can a stranger spell it after hearing it? |
| **Mean it** | Does the Sanskrit meaning actually describe *this* product — or is it decoration? |
| **Own it** | Is the word already a big company, a government portal, or a global brand? |
| **Grow it** | Will it still fit when it's not just ideas but expansion, hiring, exports, funding? |

Ones that pass all four get ⭐.

---

## The top 5 (my actual recommendation)

### 1. Manthan — मंथन — "the churning" ⭐⭐⭐

**The single best fit.** *Samudra Manthan* is the churning of the cosmic ocean: the gods and demons
churn it together and it yields **both amrita (nectar) and halahala (poison)**. That is exactly what
idea validation is — you churn an idea and you get the good and the lethal out of the same process.

- **Why it wins:** the metaphor is precise, it's short (2 syllables), easy to spell globally, and nobody who knows the story will ever forget what the product does.
- **Positioning line:** *"Churn the idea. Keep the nectar. See the poison first."*
- **Tagline options:** "Every idea, churned." · "What survived. What sank."
- **Notes:** the word is used by various firms (Manthan Systems), so verify trademarks in your class before committing. `manthan.ai` / `getmanthan.com` style domains are the realistic route.

### 2. Darpan — दर्पण — "mirror" ⭐⭐⭐

An evidence-based advisor's whole job is to **reflect reality back at you**, including the parts you
don't want to see. A mirror has no opinion — it shows what's there. That is the anti-hype promise.

- **Why it wins:** short, universally pronounceable, and it encodes your core differentiator (no flattery, no hallucinated optimism).
- **Positioning line:** *"A mirror for your business idea."*
- **Tagline:** "It won't tell you what you want. It'll tell you what's there."
- **Notes:** used by several Indian government portals (state-level "Darpan" schemes), so trademark search is essential.

### 3. Sarathi — सारथि — "charioteer" ⭐⭐⭐

In the Mahabharata, Krishna does not fight Arjuna's war — he **drives the chariot** and gives counsel
at the moment of decision. The ideal advisor archetype: not the hero, the one steering.

- **Why it wins:** beloved, warm, instantly understood across India; scales from "my business advisor,"
  to "the guide through market entry," to "the co-pilot." Works beautifully as a tagline too.
- **Positioning line:** *"You drive. I hold the reins and read the road."*
- **Tagline:** "Your sarathi for the market you're entering."
- **Notes:** also means "driver" in everyday Hindi — a mild double meaning, which is charming rather than confusing.

### 4. Udyam — उद्यम — "enterprise, the act of undertaking" ⭐⭐

The exact Sanskrit word for what your user is doing. It carries effort, risk and initiative rather
than mere "idea" — which is the right frame: your agent tests *undertakings*, not whims.

- **Why it wins:** crisp, business-native, and it will feel familiar because India's MSME registration portal is called Udyam.
- **Positioning line:** *"Before you take the leap, know the terrain."*
- **Tagline:** "Where undertakings get tested."
- **Notes:** **Udyam is the Indian government's MSME portal** — strong cultural recognition, but it will compete for search results and may be hard to trademark. Treat it as an inspiration word, not a safe brand.

### 5. Kautilya — कौटिल्य — the strategist who wrote the Arthashastra ⭐⭐

Kautilya (Chanakya) advised Chandragupta Maurya and wrote the **Arthashastra** — an ancient treatise
on economics, trade, taxation, espionage and statecraft. If any historical figure is the patron saint
of a business-strategy agent, it is him. The Arthashastra even discusses **market analysis, competitor
assessment and risk** — your exact feature set.

- **Why it wins:** instant authority. "Kautilya" signals strategic depth, not another chatbot.
- **Positioning line:** *"2,300 years of strategy, applied to your idea."*
- **Tagline:** "The Arthashastra, rebuilt for founders."
- **Notes:** heavily used in Indian education/policy (Kautilya schools, edtech, UPSC coaching), so trademark is a real obstacle. **Strong as a product-story asset even if you pick another name** — e.g. name the analysis engine "Arthashastra mode."

## Naming formula if you want something not on the list

Sanskrit gives you two reusable patterns:

1. **Root + modern suffix** → `Manthan AI` · `Darpan.app` · `Sarathi Labs` · `Vriddhi HQ`
2. **Two-root compound** → the classical way to coin new words:
   - **Arthabodh** (artha + bodh) = "understanding wealth"
   - **Nitivyakti** (niti + vyakti) = "strategy expressed"
   - **Margabodh** (marga + bodh) = "understanding the path"
   - **Vivekbodh** (vivek + bodh) = "awakened discernment"

Compounds are authentic Sanskrit practice — but test pronunciation with 5 non-Indian speakers before you commit.

## Words to avoid (and why)

| Avoid | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Maya** (illusion) | You are building an *evidence-based* agent. Naming it "illusion" is self-sabotage. |
| **Rahasya** (secret) | You promise transparency and citation. |
| **Nirvana** | Overused globally, and means *extinction/liberation* — wrong signal for a growth tool. |
| **Karma** | Universally overused; also carries fatalism, the opposite of "here's what to do next." |
| **Guru** | Overused, and invites "trust me" positioning when you want "check the evidence." |
| **Veda** | Overused by companies (Vedanta, etc.) and claims too much authority. |
| **Shanti / Anand** | Peace and bliss — lovely words, off-message for competitive strategy. |
| **Udaan** | Popular for startups already; also Hindi rather than Sanskrit. |

## Before you commit — the 30-minute checklist

1. **Say it out loud to 5 people** who don't speak Indian languages. Write down how they spell it. If two spellings come back, the name will cost you users forever.
2. **Search the trademark register** in your class: [ipindia.gov.in](https://ipindia.gov.in) (India), plus USPTO and EUIPO if you'll be global. A name can be beautiful and unusable.
3. **Search the exact name** on Google, GitHub, the app stores and [skills.sh](https://www.skills.sh). Note existing companies *and* existing Sanskrit-word brands in unrelated sectors.
4. **Grab the handle set** — GitHub org, X, LinkedIn, the `.com` and `.in` — before announcing anything.
5. **Check the negative meaning.** Many Sanskrit words have changed meaning in modern Hindi, Marathi or Bengali. Ask a native speaker of the market you're targeting, not just a Sanskritist.
6. **Write the one-line pitch with the name in it.** If it reads awkwardly, the name is wrong no matter how good the etymology is.

# Deep dive: `Shodh` and `Arthabodh` for this project

These two are the strongest *pair* on the list, because they name two different things:
**Shodh is the method. Arthabodh is the outcome.** Everything below is a careful reading of both
words against what this agent actually does.

---

## शोध · Shodh · `śodha`

### Form
| | |
|---|---|
| **Devanagari** | शोध |
| **IAST** | śodha |
| **Root** | √śudh (शुध्) — *to purify, to cleanse, to make clear* |
| **Syllables** | 1 (SHOHDH) |
| **Family** | शुद्ध *śuddha* (pure) · शुद्धि *śuddhi* (purity, correction) · शोधन *śodhana* (purification, **refining** — of metal, oil, thought) · संशोधन *saṃśodhana* (amendment, correction) · शोधक *śodhaka* (one who investigates) |

### What it literally means
Two senses, both alive in the word:

1. **Search, investigation, enquiry** — तलाश, खोज, जाँच. This is the dominant modern sense: *शोध करना* = to research; *शोध निबंध* = research paper. In Hindi and Marathi, शोध **is** the everyday word for research.
2. **Purification, clearing, refining** — from the root √śudh. This is the older sense, and it is why *śodhana* means the refining of ore into metal.

The two senses were never really separate: you **clear away** what is false, and what remains is knowledge. Research as a process of purification.

### Why it fits this project
- **The method, named exactly.** The agent does अन्वेषण (exploration), but what it actually delivers is शोध — investigation with the noise scraped off.
- **The refining sense is the real gift.** The user arrives with a rough ore of an idea. The agent refines it: separates the workable metal from the rock. That is सोना निकालना, smelting — and it is exactly the product promise.
- **Credibility by association.** ShodhGanga is the Indian government's national repository of doctoral research (INFLIBNET). "Shodh" sits inside India's research vocabulary already, so the name arrives pre-loaded with seriousness.
- **Verb-friendly.** It can be used: *"Shodh your idea."* *"Run a shodh."* That flexibility is rare — most Sanskrit nouns don't verb well in English sentences.

### Positioning lines
- *"Search. Refine. Then decide."*
- *"The research your idea deserves — before it costs you money."*
- *"शोध पहले, निवेश बाद में।"* (Research first, investment after.)

### Honest risks
| Risk | Detail | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **"Shod" is an English word** | *Shod* = fitted with horseshoes (past tense of *shoe*), and it lives one letter from *shoddy* = poor quality. English-first markets may feel a faint negative pull. | Pair it: *Shodh AI*, *Shodh Labs*, *Shodh Engine* — the qualifier removes the echo. |
| **One syllable = hard to own** | Short words are swallowed by search engines next to ShodhGanga, Shodh Samachar and similar. | Own the handle set early; use the `.ai` or `get…` route. |
| **Aspirated "dh"** | Non-Indians say *SHOD* (as in *nod*); the sound is a breathy *d*. | Acceptable compromise — the meaning survives a wrong vowel. |

## अर्थबोध · Arthabodh · `arthabodha`

### Form
| | |
|---|---|
| **Devanagari** | अर्थबोध (also written अर्थबोध / *Arthbodh*) |
| **IAST** | arthabodha |
| **Compound type** | *tatpuruṣa* — अर्थस्य बोधः *arthasya bodhaḥ* = "the understanding **of** artha" |
| **Syllables** | 4 (AR-tha-bodh) |

### Part 1 — अर्थ · artha
| | |
|---|---|
| **Root** | √arth (अर्थ्) — *to strive after, to seek, to obtain, to desire* |
| **Family** | अर्थिन् *arthin* (seeker, suitor, plaintiff) · अर्थयति *arthayati* (he seeks) |

**The root is the key insight:** *artha* literally means **"that which one strives for."** It is the object of seeking. From that single idea it grows into:

- **wealth, property, material prosperity** — the means of living well
- **purpose, goal, motive, aim**
- **meaning** — what a word "aims at"
- **affair, matter, case** (as in a legal or business matter)
- **cause, reason**

And critically: **अर्थ is one of the four पुरुषार्थ *puruṣārthas*** — dharma (duty), **artha (prosperity)**, kāma (desire), mokṣa (liberation). In Indian thought, artha is not greed. It is the **legitimate, even noble, pursuit of security and prosperity** — the material foundation that makes the other three possible. Kautilya's *Arthaśāstra* — the ancient treatise on economics, trade, taxation and strategy — takes its name from exactly this word.

For a business tool, that is a far better story than hustle culture: *wanting your business to make money is a valid life aim, not a moral failing.*

### Part 2 — बोध · bodha
| | |
|---|---|
| **Root** | √budh (बुध्) — *to wake, to awake, to perceive, to know, to understand* |
| **Family** | बुद्ध *buddha* (the awakened one) · बोधि *bodhi* (awakening) · बुद्धि *buddhi* (the intellect, the faculty of discernment) · प्रबोध *prabodha* (awakening, rousing to understanding) · बोधन *bodhana* (teaching, instructing) |

**bodha** = knowledge, comprehension, understanding, perception, waking awareness. Not information — *awakening into knowing*. It is the moment comprehension lands.

*(Small trap: बोध bodha = knowledge, while बुध budha = the planet Mercury and Wednesday, बुधवार. Non-Indians conflate the two constantly. Different words, one letter apart in roman script.)*

### Putting them together — and the double meaning that makes it work
अर्थबोध can be read two ways, and **both are true of this product**:

1. **अर्थ का बोध** — *understanding of wealth / money / business.* Comprehending the economics of an idea. This reading is already established in Hindi and Marathi, where *अर्थबोध* is used for **economic literacy** — knowing how money, trade and enterprise actually work.
2. **अर्थ का बोध** — *understanding of meaning.* Grasping what something really *means*, as opposed to what it appears to be.

Read it as either and you have described your agent: **it tells you what your idea means, and what it will do to your money.** One word, both halves of the product.

### Why it fits this project
- **It names the deliverable, not the tool.** Shodh is what the agent does; Arthabodh is what the user walks away with. That is the better name for a *product*.
- **The double meaning is on-message.** "Understanding before investment" and "know the meaning of your idea" are the same promise.
- **It inherits Kautilya's authority without his trademark baggage.** Arthashastra is taken; Arthabodh is the same intellectual lineage, unoccupied.
- **It gives the report a name.** The output of the agent can literally be *"your Arthabodh"* — a readable artefact, not a chat log. That is a product feature hiding inside a word.

### Positioning lines
- *"Understanding before investment."*
- *"What your idea means — and what it will do to your money."*
- *"अर्थ का बोध: पहले समझो, फिर निवेश करो।"*
- Report artefact name: **"Your Arthabodh"** (a PDF titled *Arthabodh — <idea name>* )

### Honest risks
| Risk | Detail | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Spelling drift** | It appears as Arthabodh / Arthbodh / Arth-Bodh / Arthabodha. Search traffic splits four ways. | Pick one spelling, register the variants as redirects, never use the others in print. |
| **Four syllables** | Longer than a one-word global brand wants to be. | Shorten in speech to **"Bodh"** for the app icon and the verb; keep Arthabodh as the formal name. |
| **"Arth" is busy** | Several Indian fintechs, NGOs and edtechs use Arth. | Don't brand on *Arth* alone — the compound is what makes it ownable. |
| **Aspirated ending** | AR-tha-bodh — non-Indians often land on *-bod* or *-bode*. | Tolerable; the *bod* ending is a soft, innocuous sound in English. |

## How to use them together — the recommendation

They are not competitors. **Use both, in different roles:**

| Layer | Name | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **The product** | **Arthabodh** | Names the outcome the user receives. Scales to every future feature: expansion, hiring, exports, funding. |
| **The engine** | **Shodh** | Names the retrieval-and-reasoning process inside. Shows up in the UI ("Running shodh… 47 case files, 19 markets"), in the docs, and in the engineering language. |
| **The cultural foundation** | **Arthashastra** | The narrative layer — Kautilya's treatise referenced in the About page and the "why this exists" story. |

Full architectural reading:

> **Arthabodh** runs a **Shodh** grounded in the **Arthashastra** tradition.

Three Sanskrit words, three real jobs, no meaning wasted. And the user-facing pitch stays simple:
***"Shodh your idea. Get your Arthabodh."*** — search your idea; receive understanding of what it
means and what it will do to your money.

### A motto, with a caveat
A constructed Sanskrit-style line: **शोधात् बोधः** *śodhāt bodhaḥ* — "from research, understanding."
This is a phrase **I have formed, not a classical quotation** — the grammar is straightforward
(ablative + nominative: understanding arises *from* research), but have a Sanskritist verify it
before printing it on anything. Never ship an invented Sanskrit phrase as though it were scripture;
that is the one mistake that will cost you respect with the exact audience you're courting.
